<div class="bio-text">
Our students already have an AI tutor, and it does their homework. This is the case for a different one. DSA is built from your own course materials, it gives hints instead of answers, and how much help it gives on a topic depends on what the student has shown on graded practice. You see your class on one page, and in a Sunday email.
</div>
<div class="bio-text">
One deck, three audiences. Part 1 is for faculty who might put a course on it: what it does for students, the learning science behind each feature, what you see as the instructor, and how much work it is. Part 2 is for administration: cost, privacy, academic integrity, and what it needs to grow. Part 3 is for other universities. Skip whichever part the room does not need. The slide I would not skip is the last one of Part 1, which is what we do not know yet: DSA has not been tested in a controlled study.
</div>

## What is in it

<ol class="talk-outline">
  <li><strong>The problem.</strong> Students ask AI for answers, unguarded answers can lower exam scores, and a prompt alone leaks.</li>
  <li><strong>Three ideas.</strong> Hints enforced by code, a tutor built from your course, and help that follows evidence.</li>
  <li><strong>Practice that counts.</strong> Mastery checks, spaced review, quick checks, find my bug, Teach-Back and reflection, each with the result behind it.</li>
  <li><strong>What you see and what you control.</strong> The dashboard, the weekly email, rosters, exams, class materials and graded rounds.</li>
  <li><strong>For administration.</strong> Estimated cost per student, caps and who pays, FERPA and student data rights, integrity, and the honest list of what is not in place yet.</li>
  <li><strong>For other universities.</strong> What travels, where it sits among the alternatives, and what adoption would need.</li>
</ol>

<div class="summary-block">
Every cost figure is an estimate from scripted development runs, not measured student use. The screenshots come from a local copy with made-up students. No production data is in the deck.
</div>

## Presenting it yourself

The slides are plain HTML with Reveal.js sitting next to them, so the folder works with no network. Arrow keys or space to advance, <code>S</code> for the speaker window (a timer and the next slide; these decks carry no notes), <code>F</code> for full screen, <code>O</code> for the slide overview, <code>B</code> to blank the screen, <code>?</code> for help. Feel free to borrow any of it.
